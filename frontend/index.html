NASA SPHEREx Space Observatory (2025-2028) 0.75 – 5.0 μm

SPHEREx (Spectro-Photometer for the History of the Universe, Epoch of Reionization, and Ices Explorer) performs an all-sky near-infrared spectral survey across 102 individual spectrophotometric bands. Equipped with 6 detector arrays, SPHEREx detects frozen water ice ($H_2O$), carbon dioxide ice ($CO_2$), and methane ($CH_4$) in interstellar clouds and maps Planet X / Brown Dwarf candidates.

NASA/IPAC Infrared Science Archive (IRSA) & 2MASS TAP / FinderChart

Hosted at Caltech/JPL, NASA IRSA serves the historical Two Micron All-Sky Survey (2MASS, 1997–2001) in $J\ (1.25\,\mu\text{m})$, $H\ (1.65\,\mu\text{m})$, and $K_s\ (2.17\,\mu\text{m})$ bands. By subtracting 2MASS from SPHEREx across a ~26.2-year temporal baseline, sub-pixel dipoles reveal slow-moving brown dwarfs and trans-Neptunian objects.

NASA Backyard Worlds: Planet 9 & WiseView Bridge byw.tools

The flagship NASA citizen science project searching for brown dwarfs and Planet Nine beyond Pluto. SPHEREx Hunter integrates a 1-click verification bridge to the official WiseView flipbook tool developed by Dan Caselden and the Backyard Worlds science team.

Canadian Space Agency (CSA) NEOSSat Satellite CADC Collection

NEOSSat (Near-Earth Object Surveillance Satellite) is Canada's dedicated microsatellite operated by the Canadian Space Agency (CSA) and DRDC. Tracking asteroids, comets, and satellites, NEOSSat optical data serves as the primary discriminator to distinguish fast-moving Near-Earth Asteroids ($>15''/\text{hour}$) from slow Planet X brown dwarf candidates ($<10''/\text{year}$).

Canadian Astronomy Data Centre (CADC / NRC Herzberg) National Facility

Canada's primary scientific astronomical repository hosted at the National Research Council Herzberg Astronomy and Astrophysics Research Centre in Victoria, British Columbia. Distributes official NEOSSat FITS frames and solar system ephemeris cross-matching.

ESA Gaia DR3 & CDS Strasbourg Aladin / SIMBAD 1.8B Sources

The European Space Agency Gaia Data Release 3 catalog provides ultra-high precision astrometry, 50-year proper motion vectors, and parallaxes. Coupled with CDS Strasbourg SIMBAD metadata, it instantly verifies optical stars vs infrared-exclusive brown dwarf dipoles.

1. Alard-Lupton Numerical Difference Imaging
D(x, y) = I₂(x, y) - S · [I₁ ⊗ K](x, y)

$I_2$ is the science epoch (SPHEREx), $I_1$ is the reference epoch (2MASS), $K$ is the spatially-varying Point Spread Function (PSF) matching kernel ($\sigma=1.15$), and $S$ is the photometric scale factor. Background cancels to neutral mid-gray (128), leaving stationary stars invisible and moving objects as black/white dipole pairs.

2. Total Proper Motion Vector & Position Angle
μ = √[ (μ_α cos δ)² + μ_δ² ] | θ = atan2(μ_α cos δ, μ_δ)

Measures total angular shift across the celestial sphere in milliarcseconds per year (mas/yr). A companion dipole separation $d$ over baseline $\Delta t$ directly yields proper motion speed.

3. Planet X vs Asteroid vs Red Dwarf Discrimination Criteria
• Brown Dwarf / Planet X: Extreme IR color (G - Ks > 4.5, J - Ks > 1.2), no optical Gaia DR3 match, annual motion (0.1 - 10 "/yr).
• Near-Earth Asteroid (NEO): Rapid diurnal streak (> 15 "/hour), optical detection in NEOSSat, fast transient in single exposure.
• Normal Field Star: G - Ks < 3.0, Gaia DR3 confirmed with zero dipole residual in difference map.
